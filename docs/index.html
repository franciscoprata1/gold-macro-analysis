Question 1

Does money printing drive the gold price?

Over decades, partly yes. Year to year, no. Gold has outgrown both the money supply and consumer prices since 1971, so it has preserved purchasing power. But the timing does not follow M2: from 1980 to 2001 the money supply more than tripled while gold lost 61% of its value.

  • CPI tracks gold slightly better than M2 does (0.30 yearly correlation), which fits gold's role as an inflation hedge rather than a money-supply mirror.
Question 2

How does gold relate to bond yields?

Through real yields, not nominal ones. The real yield is the 10-year Treasury rate minus inflation, the return a bond pays after inflation. When it falls, holding gold (which pays nothing) costs less, and gold tends to rise. Nominal yields alone show no relationship.

Question 3

Do gold and the S&P 500 move together?

Almost independently, which is why they diversify each other. Their monthly returns have essentially zero correlation over 55 years. Instead they take turns: stocks dominated the 1980s and 1990s, gold the 1970s and 2000s.

Question 4

What does a house cost in gold, and in stocks?

In money, houses got 11 to 50 times more expensive. In gold, they are cheaper today than in 1975 everywhere we measured. But gold only protected you over long periods: the price of a house in ounces swung more than sixfold, so when you bought the gold mattered a lot. Stocks did better over the full 50 years, but gold has won for most starting years since the late 1990s.

  • Timing dominates. Gold bought in 2000 or 2005 now buys four to eight times more house. Gold bought at the 1980 peak buys about the same house in the US, and less in the UK and Spain.
  • Today is unusual. After gold's rally since 2024, a US house is the cheapest in gold of the whole period, and UK and Spanish houses the cheapest since the early 1980s. Put the other way, gold is historically expensive relative to housing.
  • Stocks or gold depends on the start year. Savings started from 1975 to 1995, or from 2009 to 2013, bought more house in the S&P 500. Savings started from 1996 to 2008, or from 2014 onwards, bought more in gold. This uses the price index only; with dividends, stocks would win more often.

House prices. US: Census median price of new houses sold. UK: HM Land Registry average of £268,000 in January 2026, taken back in time with the BIS UK property price index. Euro area: BIS index only, since there is no official average price; before 1999 the Deutsche Mark stands in for the euro. Exchange rates: FRED January spot rates.
